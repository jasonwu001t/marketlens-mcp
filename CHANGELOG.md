# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). The canonical schema (`SCHEMA_VERSION`) and the plugin API (`PLUGIN_API_VERSION`) are versioned separately.

## [0.2.0] - Unreleased

### Added

- `news_official` (capability `news`, on by default), from a new built-in provider, `official`, registered after `data`: official headlines, live (no history), from a fixed allow-list of 18 RSS/Atom feeds of the Federal Reserve Board (`fed.monetary`, `fed.press`, `fed.speeches`, `fed.testimony`), SEC (`sec.press`, `sec.speeches`, `sec.litigation`), BLS (`bls.latest`, `bls.cpi`, `bls.empsit`, `bls.ppi`, `bls.jolts`, `bls.eci`), BEA (`bea.news`), CFTC (`cftc.press`, `cftc.enforcement`, `cftc.speeches`) and ECB (`ecb.press`), as `marketlens.NewsItem` rows marked untrusted. Filters: `publishers`, `feeds` (unknown ids refused), `start`/`end`/`lookback` (default P7D), `query` (whole words in headline or summary), `sort`. `news_id` is `<feed id>:<hash>`, so `results_query` can filter by feed; an item in two feeds is kept once, under the first of the allow-list. The SEC and BLS feeds are read only with `SEC_CONTACT_EMAIL`, sent in the User-Agent to them alone; without it (or when it is not an email address) they are skipped with a note, and no placeholder contact is ever sent. GET only; a redirect is followed only to https on the publisher's own hosts; at most 5 MB per feed, uncompressed; a document with a DOCTYPE or ENTITY declaration, or in an encoding expat does not read, is refused; HTML, escaped or sent as XML elements, is reduced to text with `html.parser`, script and style dropped; links resolve against the document's `xml:base`; a link that is not https on the publisher's own domain, or that holds a backslash, is withheld (BEA's http links are served as https); an item without a date that parses is skipped and counted. `author` is the feed's dc:creator or Atom author where that names one (the Fed's feeds name none). A failed feed, for whatever reason, leaves a partial answer with a note; only when every feed fails is the call an error. As the Alpaca client, a timeout or a lost connection fails a feed at once and a 5xx is retried once; a 429 fails the feed at once; the whole call stops 10 seconds after the request timeout. Each feed is kept in process for 300 seconds, and the ECB's robots.txt Crawl-delay (5 s) holds even with the cache off. Settings `providers.official.cache_seconds` and `timeout_seconds`; `doctor` says how many feeds are ready. The feeds left out, and why (robots.txt, no feed, stale, or terms that forbid this use, as for WSJ, CNBC and the FT), are in `providers/official/feeds.py` and the README.
- The `data` extra, marketlens-data installed beside marketlens-mcp (Python 3.11 or later; `pip install "marketlens-mcp[data]"` once both are on PyPI, see the README's Install section): a built-in provider over marketlens-data (import name `omni`) for official data, 25 tools under five new capabilities:
  - `macro` (on): FRED/ALFRED series with vintages and `as_of` reads (a value is knowable from 17:00 St. Louis time, 22:00 UTC or 23:00 in winter, of the day FRED posted the release it first appeared in, a revision from the release that revised it; for a series FRED posts after its ALFRED date, such as the ICE BofA spreads, VIX or U. Michigan's survey, that later day), BLS monthly and quarterly series (a quarter, such as the Employment Cost Index's, is dated at its first day with frequency `Q` and kept when any of its months is in the window; a value is knowable from the latest release in BLS's own release archive at the sync that first saw it, never before its month's own release, 08:30 ET or JOLTS 10:00 ET, CES real earnings with the CPI or a later jobs report, which revises them, and a seasonally adjusted CPI between December's CPI and January's, when BLS publishes the revised seasonal factors on a day of their own, from the sync, as is a value seen while BLS's archive page cannot be read, since BLS serves only the latest revision: history older than the first sync is knowable from that sync's latest release, so an earlier `as_of` returns none of it, and a revision seen later is a new vintage from the release it came with, else from the sync that saw it), BEA NIPA tables (a `Level` value's `units` carry BEA's measure and multiplier, `Level, millions of Current Dollars`; one stored without the multiplier, before marketlens-data kept it, is `Level` alone with a note that its scale is not known; a value is knowable from its table's last revision date, never before BEA's first release of its period, since BEA serves only the latest revision: history older than the first sync is knowable from that sync's last revision date, so an earlier `as_of` returns none of it, and a revision seen later is a new vintage from its date), BLS release schedules, the FRED series catalogue.
  - `filings` (on): SEC EDGAR filings, XBRL facts with restatement vintages, point-in-time fundamentals, 8-K Item 2.02 earnings releases and press-release EPS, Form 4 and 144 insider transactions, 13F holdings, fund N-PORT reports and holdings.
  - `fed_treasury` (on): FOMC meetings and statements, NY Fed reference rates (SOFR, EFFR, OBFR, TGCR, BGCR), the Treasury par yield curve, Treasury auctions (an auction's results knowable from 13:30 ET on its day, after its announcement), debt to the penny and the Treasury General Account.
  - `holidays` (on): US market holidays and early closes from NYSE, SIFMA and OPM, keyless; with Alpaca keys NYSE's dates are cross-checked against Alpaca's trading calendar, and without them the answer says they were not.
  - `calendars` (off: the Nasdaq endpoint is unofficial): the economic calendar with PMI and other actual and consensus figures, the earnings calendar and earnings history.
- Each source names its own key (`FRED_API_KEY`, `BEA_API_KEY`; `BLS_API_KEY` optional; `SEC_CONTACT_EMAIL` optional but strongly recommended for SEC fair access: without it marketlens-data sends a placeholder contact and every `sec_` answer carries a note saying so; set to something that is not an email address, the `sec_` tools refuse in mode `auto` until it is fixed; the rest keyless); `doctor` reports which sources are ready, where the data store is and in which mode.
- `providers.data` settings: `mode` (`auto` fetches and keeps, `local` reads only what is stored), `data_dir`, `ttl_hours`, `calendar_ttl_minutes`, `call_timeout_seconds`.
- Schema 1.1.0: 23 canonical models for the data tools and the units `as_published`, `USD_per_share`, `times`, `ordinal`.
- The README tool table lists the data tools whether or not the extra is installed, marked "needs the data extra".
- `market_movers` takes `min_price` (USD) and, for stocks, `exclude_warrants_rights_units`, judged by symbol (five letters ending in W, R or U, Nasdaq's fifth letter: NRSNW, CHARR, CCAQU; or a WS, RT or U suffix: AAC-WS, BCAT-RT) and by asset name (see Fixed). With either, Alpaca's top 50 gainers and losers are screened, kept in Alpaca's order, ranked from 1 among the kept and cut to `top`, and the notes say how many were dropped for which reason, naming only the directions the filters dropped movers from, and when the filters are why fewer than `top` are left. A direction Alpaca itself ranked fewer than `top` of is noted apart, not blamed on the filters: "Alpaca ranked 2 gainers and no losers, fewer than the 10 asked for." Nor are records skipped for not fitting the model, which have their own note; a shortfall the filters share with them names both: "Only 1 of 4 gainers fit the model and passed the filters." Without either filter Alpaca is asked for `top` as before and the rows are unchanged.
- Two reference tools (capability `reference`) decode the code columns of quotes and trades: `reference_stock_exchanges` (stock exchange codes, `StockMetaExchanges`) and `reference_condition_codes` (trade or quote condition codes for stocks, per tape A, B or C and all three by default, or for options, `StockMetaConditions` and `OptionMetaConditions`). Schema 1.1.0 (unreleased) gains the models `StockExchange` and `ConditionCode`. The three operations leave the 1.x candidates in `exclusions.py`.
- A dynamic table (`ToolOutput(table=...)` with a schema name for `model`) labels a column's unit in its Arrow field's metadata (`{b"unit": b"percent"}`), and the result's columns carry it, inline or stored. The plugin API is unchanged: an older server ignores the metadata. The analytics tools read those units as they read a model's: a column labelled `price`, `USD` or `percent_of_par` is a price, so `analytics_correlation` correlates its simple returns rather than its levels, and one labelled `fraction` or `fraction_per_year` is a ratio, which `analytics_returns`, `analytics_drawdown` and `analytics_beta` refuse.
- `news_search` takes `query`: only articles whose headline or summary has every word of it, as whole words in any case, with the HTML entities Alpaca's text carries read as their characters (`Wednesday&#39;s` matches `Wednesday's`). Alpaca's news API has no text search, so the server filters the articles Alpaca returns for the window and tickers (the fetch still reads them all, and the fetch limits apply before the filter), and a note says how many of how many matched, and when the fetch stopped before the end of the window. Without tickers Alpaca has several hundred articles a day, so the description asks for tickers or a window of a day or less.
- `market_most_active` rows carry each stock's `price` (latest trade), `change`, `change_pct` and `change_basis` from its snapshot (one `GET /v2/stocks/snapshots` for the list, on the configured `stock_feed`, computed as `market_snapshots` does; when the snapshots cannot be read the columns are null with a note), and the tool takes `min_price`: Alpaca's top 100 are screened, ranked from 1 among the kept and cut to `top`, and notes say how many were dropped (below the price, or without one) and any shortfall. `MostActive` gains the four optional columns (schema 1.1.0, unreleased).
- `market_movers` takes `quote_currency` for crypto (USD, USDT, USDC, BTC, ETH or EUR): only pairs quoted in it are kept (Alpaca's list has BAT/USD, BAT/USDC and UNI/BTC side by side), screening Alpaca's top 50 like the other filters.
- `crypto_trades` takes `limit`: at most that many trades per pair in the window, the first in sort order (`sort="desc", limit=100` is each pair's latest 100), one request per pair since Alpaca's own limit counts across the pairs of a request; it is refused together with `page_token`.
- Every analytics tool takes `store`: `store=true` keeps even a small output as a stored result and returns its marker, so a one-row `analytics_drawdown` or `analytics_volatility` answer can feed `results_query` or another analytics tool. `analytics_volatility` takes `window=all` (one row per series: the volatility of all its returns, with `window` the number of returns) and `input_kind` (`prices` or `returns`): a column of returns, such as a portfolio's from `results_query`, is used as it is instead of being read as prices (which skipped its negative values and took returns of returns). By default a returns result, or a column a returns result computed carried into a query answer or an aligned result, is read as returns; the `fraction` columns of other models, such as a drawdown or a beta, need `input_kind=returns`, also when carried through `analytics_align` or a query of a query. Which model computed a carried column is recorded in its `source_model` (a new optional `ColumnInfo` field, shown only where set) when `results_query`, `analytics_align` or `analytics_resample` creates the result (never as a query answer's or aligned result's own model, which computed no such column), so it is known after that result has expired or was dropped; a `fraction` column of a query answer or aligned result with no `source_model` (a dataset's, or one stored before this change) is refused without `input_kind`, naming it. A price column with no unit whose non-positive values are skipped gets a note to pass `input_kind=returns`. A `fraction_per_year` column is still refused, and `analytics_returns`, `analytics_drawdown` and `analytics_beta` still refuse ratio columns, whose values are not price levels.
- `analytics_beta` takes `result_id` like the other analytics tools, with `benchmark_series` naming the benchmark's series in it (`result_id=<bars of AAPL, MSFT, SPY>, benchmark_series=SPY`; the other series are the assets), and `benchmark_series` also picks the benchmark out of a `benchmark_result_id` with several series; `asset_result_id` with `benchmark_result_id` works as before. Rolling rows (`BetaPoint`) carry `alpha`, `r_squared`, `n_obs` and the window's `start`, as the static row does (four optional fields; the schema version stays 1.1.0, unreleased). A note counts, per series, the returns between shared timestamps that skip observations only one side has (BTC/USD, which trades around the clock, against QQQ in session: the overnight returns), suggesting `period`.
- `results_query` refusals carry a hint (the error's `hint` field; the message is unchanged): a keyword used as a bare name (a WITH name, alias or column such as `map`, `end`, `left`, `group` or `union`), which sqlglot or DuckDB refuses with a bare syntax error, is named with the fix (rename it or double-quote it), and DuckDB's grouping errors (a column neither grouped nor aggregated, `GROUP BY ALL` over an expression mixing an aggregate and a column, an aggregate in `WHERE`, nested aggregates) say how to group or aggregate. `QueryRefused` gains a `hint` attribute (its constructor is unchanged).
- `filings_search` (capability `filings`, built in: the `official` provider; it needs `SEC_CONTACT_EMAIL` and refuses without it, `official_contact_missing`): SEC EDGAR's full-text search of filings since 2001, live, one `marketlens.FilingSearchHit` row per matching document (a filing's main file or an exhibit): `accession_number`, `form`, `cik` and `entity_name` (the first entity EDGAR lists; `ciks` has all), `filing_date`, `report_date`, the document's name, type and URL, and the filing's index URL. It takes `query` in EDGAR's syntax (every word required, "exact phrase", OR, -word, word*), `forms` (a form finds its amendments: 13F-HR finds 13F-HR/A), `ciks` of 1-10 digits (1350694 is sent as 0001350694), `start`/`end` filing dates (an omitted start is 2001-01-01 and an omitted end today in New York; a start after the end, given or by default, is refused before any request), `limit` (default 100, at most 500) and `page_token`; at least one of `query`, `forms` or `ciks`. Rows are in EDGAR's order: by relevance with words, else newest filing first (main documents only); EDGAR serves only the first 10,000 hits of a search, and a note says so. Each call makes one GET per 100 hits, one at a time, at most 5, under a process-wide limiter that sends at most 5 at once and then one a second (so calls made at once stay below SEC's 10 a second), with no cache, asking for an uncompressed answer of at most 5 MB and following no redirect, under the call's deadline: a later page that fails gives the hits read so far, a note and a `page_token`; EDGAR's own refusals (HTTP 200 with an error) come back as `official_rejected`. `knowledge_time` is the end of the filing date in New York (the search states no acceptance time). Schema 1.1.0 (unreleased) gains `FilingSearchHit`. The `filings` capability's description says the `sec_` tools need the `data` extra and `filings_search` does not.

### Changed

- `NewsItem.publisher`'s description no longer names Alpaca: it is the outlet or publisher that issued the item, as the provider names its source (a description only; the schema stays 1.1.0). The plugin name `official` is reserved for the built-in provider.
- Linux and macOS are the tested platforms: the classifiers name them instead of "OS Independent", and Windows left the CI matrix (not yet supported). Text I/O still names its encoding everywhere.
- A fetch cut at `fetch.max_rows` or `fetch.max_pages` without a page token no longer carries the R16 "call again with page_token" note: the tool's own truncation note stands, and a tool that gave none gets "The data is incomplete; narrow the request." (the answer is still flagged `truncated`).
- The name `data` is reserved for the built-in provider; a plugin may not take it.
- `market_snapshots` and `crypto_snapshots` give the session's change: `change` and `change_pct` come from the last trade only while it falls in the latest session (a stock's day bar's regular hours, 09:30-16:00 New York, without an extended-hours condition T or U; a crypto pair's current UTC day bar), and otherwise from `day_close` against `prev_close`, with a note naming the tickers. Before, an after-hours print (NVDA: -2.83 % instead of the session's -2.94 %) or a weeks-old crypto trade (ETH/BTC: +4.9 % on a day it moved -0.13 %) made the change. The new optional column `change_basis` (`last_trade` or `day_close`) says which, and the `Snapshot` columns `last_price`, `day_*`, `prev_close`, `change` and `change_pct` have descriptions (schema 1.1.0, unreleased, gains the column).
- `fixed_income_latest_quotes` says in its description and README line that it needs an Alpaca plan that includes fixed income, which most plans do not, and that without it every call fails with `alpaca_not_entitled`. The server still does no I/O at start or registration, so it does not probe the plan to hide the tool.
- `results_query` lowers a `max_rows` above `results.query_max_rows` (200 by default) to it, with a note, as it lowers a larger `LIMIT`, instead of refusing it as invalid arguments; the description and README name the limits: no `PIVOT` or `UNPIVOT` (now `sql_forbidden` with a conditional-aggregation example wherever it appears; a top-level `PIVOT` was `sql_not_select` and a nested one `sql_forbidden` with no way round), at most 8 results a query, and an answer over 24,000 bytes of JSON (`results.query_max_bytes`) or about 6,000 tokens is stored, with a note that says which limit, the answer's size and how to see it inline.
- pydantic 2.12 or later is required (was 2.11): `ColumnInfo` leaves out an unset `source_model` with the field's `exclude_if`, so a column's JSON Schema still lists its fields in serialization mode.
- `news_search` with `query` and no `tickers` refuses at once, before any request, a window that holds more articles than one call reads: Alpaca has about 850 a day across all tickers, and a call reads `fetch.max_pages` pages of 50 (1,000 by default, so about 28 hours). The refusal (`invalid_arguments`) says how many the window holds and hints to give tickers or a window of at most N hours, or to use `filings_search` when the `filings` capability is on. Before, it read 20 pages of the newest articles and could report no match for a name the rest of the window held. A query with tickers, or one continuing with `page_token`, is unchanged.

### Fixed

- `market_bars`, `options_bars` and `crypto_bars` return no trades after `end`. Alpaca returns every bar that starts by `end` whole, so the 1w bar for `end` 2026-10-08, a Thursday, held Friday's trades (AAPL: Friday's close 336.64 and the week's volume 172,990,628 against 134,952,290 for Monday to Thursday) while `provenance.as_of` said 2026-10-08; 1mo, Nmo, 1d and intraday bars did the same for an `end` inside their period. For a bar whose period runs past an `end` before the newest data, one request for the trades from `end` to the bar's close (on the bars' feed) says whether anything trades after `end`: if nothing does, the bar is whole as Alpaca sent it, `vwap` included (a week whose `end` is a Friday; a 1d bar whose `end` is 20:00 New York only when nothing prints after 20:00, as for AAPL on the SIP feed but not SPY, whose official-close prints are stamped 20:00:00.015, so an `end` given as a date, which takes in the whole day, keeps both). Otherwise a 1w or Nmo bar is rebuilt from the 1d bars of its whole days to `end` (one more request: open, high, low, close, volume and trade count, which Alpaca's longer bars are of their days; `vwap` null, `insufficient_data`), and a 1d or shorter one, which only trades would rebuild exactly, is left out; a note names each such bar, where its period ends, that trades after `end` fall in it ("may fall" when the trades request failed or did not show every instrument), and the dates it covers or why it was left out (its 1d bars failed or stopped at the fetch limits). When the window reaches the newest data, or a bar kept whole runs past it (AAPL's October 1mo bar for `end` 2026-10-16 asked for on Saturday 10-17, which says "(none after end)"), the current bar is kept and named, for stocks and options as for crypto, without calling its trades partial (a stock's week asked for on a Saturday is over): "The 1w bar from 2026-10-12T04:00:00Z (AAPL) is the current one: its period runs to 2026-10-19T04:00:00Z, after now (...), so it holds the trades so far, and any later trade in its period would change it." A delayed feed's newest bar whose period ended before now (an option's 5min bar 15 minutes back) is "the newest one served", running past "the newest data the feed serves", not past now. Periods follow the New York calendar for stocks and options (a week across the November clock change ends at 05:00Z) and UTC for crypto. Alpaca leaves out a bar that starts before `start`, so no bar holds trades before it, and a note now names the stretch from `start` that no bar covers: "Alpaca leaves out the 1w bar from 2026-10-05T04:00:00Z, which starts before start (2026-10-07T00:00:00Z): no bar covers start to 2026-10-12T04:00:00Z." (Nmin and Nh bars start at multiples of N from 0001-01-01T00:00Z, so 7min bars at 13:34Z; a start at 00:00 UTC means that day in New York for stocks and options, and a lookback's start for intraday bars is not named.)
- `options_chain` with `expiration` and `expiration_from` or `expiration_to` returns that expiration's contracts: Alpaca's chain endpoint takes an `expiration_date` sent beside a range as the range's other bound (AAPL `expiration=2026-11-20` with `expiration_from=2026-11-01` gave the 412 contracts of 11-06, 11-13 and 11-20 instead of 11-20's 168), so the exact date is now sent alone. An `expiration` outside the range is refused, as an inverted range is, by `options_chain` and `reference_option_contracts` alike: "expiration (2026-11-20) must not be before expiration_from (2026-12-01)".
- `sec_insider_trades`: a Form 144 row's `price_per_share` is null, `not_applicable`. A notice states the shares to be sold and their aggregate market value, no price, and the price it carried was the value over the shares. `InsiderTransaction` says a Form 144 row is one line of its notice, a line per securitiesInformation block it lists, each a class of security and a broker, which can recur (marketlens-data reads every one since its fix of 2026-10-16; it read the first), and that a Form 144's `has_10b5_1` is the plan adoption date it gives.
- `analytics_align` is point in time on a right result with a column whose role is `available` (when each row became known, published or first available, beside the time column the row describes; a new optional `ColumnInfo.role`, shown only where set, which a built-in model states in its field's JSON Schema, `x-role`, as every official-data model's `knowledge_time` now does, a dynamic table marks in its Arrow field metadata, `{b"role": b"available"}`, and a stored query answer keeps on such a column it passes through unchanged, with its name and type, or builds as `max()` or `greatest()` of such columns from every result it read that has one, whatever its time column, and with each row computed from the rows it keeps the clocks of): a right row counts from the later of its time and that column, when it has both happened and become known, so a left row gets the right row with the latest time at or before its time among those known by then (backward), or the first update at or after it (forward), with a note naming the clock; a schedule or calendar known in advance still gives the last date at or before the left row, never the next one; the tolerance is measured to that clock, and right rows without it are left out with a note. Before, a point-in-time dataset's rows were matched on their event time, so a bar received a value published after it (259 of 260 SPY daily bars aligned with the 10-year Treasury yield got the next evening's value). `match_on=time` matches on the time column as before, with a note counting the look-ahead matches. A revision of an older time is no update: the latest time known stays matched, in its latest version then. A note says how much older the matches are than the latest right time by the time column (the publication lag, or more), and when the right result holds one version of each time and some of them were revised (became known after a later time did), a note says a revised row is there only from its revision, so a newer value may have been known at the left time in a version the result does not hold: SPY's daily bars of 2024-01 to 2026-10 aligned onto payrolls (PAYEMS) held in their latest vintage get months a median of 1,371 days older than the latest month at the bar (January 2026's bar gets January 2022, the newest month the February 2026 benchmark revision did not re-stamp), which went unsaid before. A stored query answer that leaves such a column out, or computes it otherwise (`min()` of a week's knowledge times is earlier than some of the week's values), says so and keeps no role; so does one whose query computes rows from other rows, some known later (a window function such as a full-sample z-score or `lead()`, a subquery in an expression or a `LATERAL` subquery, a `SEMI` or `ANTI` join, a result read twice for one row), or joins a result dated by its time column, such as quotes, without passing that time through as the answer's time column or taking `greatest()` of it and the clock, or joins bars at all (an `ASOF JOIN` forward from each row gave the row a later bar's close; one backward, with `greatest(knowledge_time, t)`, gave a row known at 13:30 UTC that day's close, since a bar is known only at its end, which no column of the answer shows), where the role was kept beside such values and `analytics_align` called the match point in time; and so does one reading a result that does not show when its rows became known: a computed result without the role (a z-score answer that lost it, joined back to the dataset's `knowledge_time`, got the role again, and so did a full-sample mean stored apart and cross-joined, or a daily high of bars stored without a time column and joined forward, so `analytics_align` called look-ahead values point in time), a plugin's rows dated by a time column without one, rows dated with no time column, rows that can hold what became known after their time (an order created on January 5 and filled on January 12, joined forward, gave the fill to January 5's bar under a note that none was published after it; a news item's update; an account activity), or rows with no date column, which are as of their fetch (today's position P&L or an asset's status cross-joined to the dataset, or a plugin's period held as text joined forward, kept the role). Only fetched rows known by their time column and fixed from then on are taken as known by it: quotes and trades at it, bars at their end, `t` plus their timeframe (a built-in model's `span_column`), and only unadjusted (bars fetched with `adjustment=all`, `market_bars`' default, are rescaled for the splits and dividends after their time, so a value divided by a close kept the role); and only fetched reference codes (exchanges, condition codes) as holding at every time: a built-in model states it (`known_at_time`). A query answer joining quotes that passes their time through takes it as its time column. `analytics_align` onto a right result with a `knowledge_time` or `ingested_at` column but no column with the role (a query answer passing it through beside a result that does not show when its rows became known, an analytics output carrying it) matches from the later of each row's time and that column all the same, as a lower bound, and says the match is not shown point in time (`match_on=time` counts, at least, the left rows that got a row known after them); onto any other right result without such a column, it says its time-column match is not shown point in time (and which clock to keep, when it was computed from a result with the role), unless the right holds fetched rows known at their time (quotes, trades): a plugin's rows dated by the period they describe (a macro series aligned onto bars got a period's value weeks before its release without a word), a query answer, an analytics output, orders or news. `analytics_align` matches fetched bars from each bar's end, `t` plus its timeframe on the calendar the bar starts in (New York for stocks and options, so a week across a clock change is an hour shorter or longer in UTC; UTC for crypto), when its close became known, not from `t`: SPY's hourly bar of 2026-10-08T08:00Z got that day's daily close, 773.93, about 12 hours before the session ended, without a word; `match_on=time` matches on `t` and counts the bars that ended after the left row, and bars adjusted after their time are matched from their end all the same, with a note that the match is not shown point in time. So bars of one timeframe on both sides pair each left bar with the right bar before it (SPY's day bar of 2026-10-06 gets QQQ's of 10-05, where it got 10-06's), `tolerance=PT0S` gives the right bar that ended at the left time, not the one starting then, and `direction=forward` gives the first bar that ends at or after the left time, whose `matched_t` can be before it; `match_on=time` pairs bars of the same `t`, as before, counting the look-ahead. - A result whose time column is a naive TIMESTAMP (`SELECT t::TIMESTAMP AS ts, ...` with `store=true`) is stored: the marker's time span, which is of UTC instants, was filled with zone-less values and failed validation, so `analytics_align` of such a result failed as `internal_error` whenever its output was stored. The span is left empty for a naive time column, whose min and max stay in `columns`.
- The analytics tools take a result whose time column is a DATE, as the data models' macro series, yield curve, reference rates and auctions record, reading a date as its midnight UTC: every analytics tool refused them as having 'no time column'. A time column of another type is refused naming its type.
- `analytics_align` and `analytics_resample` of a dynamic result follow `results.inline_max_rows` and `inline_max_tokens` when they decide to store their output, which keeps its time and series columns: they compared against the defaults (200 rows, 6,000 tokens), so with a lower setting the server stored the output itself, without either column (the next `analytics_resample` averaged every ticker into one series under one label, with no warning), and with a higher one stored an output that fits inline.
- A fetch through `ctx.paginate` whose rows were cut at `fetch.max_rows` with no page token left (an upstream that ignored `page_limit` on its last page, or answers in one response) is `pagination.complete=false`: it was reported complete, so the server's truncation note never fired and `provenance.truncated` stayed false for a tool that wrote no note of its own.
- A stored query answer of one result keeps its default value column after that result is dropped or has expired (it always expires first): the analytics read the parent's model live, so `analytics_returns`, `analytics_drawdown` and `analytics_beta` refused the answer with `column_required` once the parent was gone. They now fall back to the model its columns record (`close`, carried from bars).
- `config init` and the result store make owner-only (0700) only the folders they create: a `MARKETLENS_CONFIG` in an existing project folder made that folder owner-only, and so did every stored result to an existing `MARKETLENS_CACHE_DIR` (one owned by another user failed every put). The store's own `results` folder and session folders stay owner-only, as do the data store and config folders marketlens creates.
- Result-store eviction deletes only the files the store writes (`r_<id>.parquet`, `r_<id>.json` and their temp names) and never follows a symbolic link: it deleted every file older than an hour in any folder under `<cache>/results`, whatever its name, so a `MARKETLENS_CACHE_DIR` pointed at a folder of the owner's own lost its `results/` subtree at start and every 10 minutes.
- `results.query_memory_limit` below 64MB is refused at start with one line ("results.query_memory_limit must be at least 64MB (got '10KB')."): `0GB` or `10KB` passed, and then every stored result, `results_query` and analytics call failed with DuckDB's out-of-memory error as `internal_error`.
- `results_export` and `results_drop` are annotated as the writes they are (`readOnlyHint=false`, `destructiveHint=true`; `results_export` also `idempotentHint=false`): every tool was annotated read-only, so a client that runs read-only tools without asking would let the model replace a file in the export folder (`overwrite=true`) or delete a stored result unasked. Every other tool, and every plugin's, stays read-only.
- `results_export` to CSV of a result marked untrusted (news headlines and summaries) writes a text cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return with a leading `'`, with a note counting them: such a cell was written as is, and a spreadsheet opening the file ran it as a formula (`=HYPERLINK(...)`). Other results and Parquet exports are unchanged.
- The result store's work no longer runs on the server's event loop: a `results_query` running to its timeout (10 s by default, up to 120 s) froze every other tool call, every other HTTP session, MCP cancellations and the upstream fetches in flight. `results_query`, `results_sample` and the analytics tools run in one query thread, one at a time (so concurrent queries never multiply `results.query_memory_limit`); `results_list`, `results_describe`, `results_drop`, `results_export` and the storing of every tool's answer run in other threads, so they are not queued behind a slow query. A stored answer's summary is a DuckDB connection under the same limit, so it can hold memory beside a query.
- A plugin named in `plugins.enabled` that fails to load no longer stops the server when the config sets its capability (`capabilities: {myvendor_data: true}`, as the README shows): the capability was refused as unknown, so the server exited with code 2 and `marketlens-mcp plugins` failed with the same refusal instead of showing why the plugin did not load. While such a plugin is missing, a configured capability nothing declares is skipped with a start-up notice naming the plugins that did not load; with every listed plugin loaded, an unknown capability is still refused.
- WRITING_A_PLUGIN.md's example `pyproject.toml` depends on `marketlens-mcp>=0.2,<0.3`: it said `>=0.1,<0.2`, which excludes this release, so a plugin copied from the guide could not be installed next to the server. A test checks that the pin admits the package's version.
- `portfolio_locate_quotes` describes `tickers` as 1-100, the most it accepts: it inherited "(1-200)" from the shared ticker list, so a model that sent 101 to 200 tickers got `invalid_arguments`.
- The `provider_docs_*` tools no longer list the Alpaca key variables in the README's tool table or in `doctor`: they send no key, but the Alpaca provider declared both key pairs for every one of its tools. Each Alpaca data and brokerage tool now declares them itself, so with only `provider.docs` on, `doctor` names no Alpaca key.
- `config show` reports `APCA_API_KEY_ID` and `APCA_API_SECRET_KEY` as set or unset: with only that spelling of the Alpaca key pair set, which the Alpaca tools accept, it said `ALPACA_API_KEY` and `ALPACA_SECRET_KEY` were unset and named nothing else. The README's configuration section says either pair works.
- `doctor --network` calls Alpaca's clock with whichever key pair is complete, as the Alpaca tools do: with only `APCA_API_KEY_ID` and `APCA_API_SECRET_KEY` set it skipped the call ("ALPACA_API_KEY and ALPACA_SECRET_KEY are not both set") and exited 1, although every Alpaca tool would work. Without either pair, the skip names both spellings.
- `doctor`'s `data source nyse` line reads either spelling of the Alpaca key pair, as marketlens-data's NYSE cross-check does: with only `APCA_API_KEY_ID` and `APCA_API_SECRET_KEY` set it said `ALPACA_API_KEY` and `ALPACA_SECRET_KEY` were not set while the cross-check ran. The README's keys table names both pairs.
- `doctor` without the data extra, and a data tool's `data_extra_missing` refusal, say to install marketlens-data beside marketlens-mcp and point to the README's Install section: they said to install `marketlens-mcp[data]`, which cannot resolve while neither package is on PyPI.
- `marketlens-mcp add-capability` writes code that passes `make lint` as written (its tuples and model names were in single quotes, which `ruff format --check` rejects), and names a new Alpaca tool's fixture by its operation, `tests/alpaca/fixtures/<operationId>.json`, empty and without a `_synthetic` key, as the fixtures' README names and marks them: it wrote `<tool_name>.json` holding `{"_synthetic": true}`. When the operation has a fixture already (`Clock.json`, `StockSnapshots.json`), it is left as it is and the new test reads it, and the next steps say to reuse it or add an `<operationId>__<case>.json`.
- `marketlens-mcp add-capability ... --package PKG --root DIR`, which writes a plugin's tool into the plugin's own folder, prints the plugin's next steps: "Add the SPECS of src/PKG/tools/NAME.py with registry.add_tool in your plugin's register function", as WRITING_A_PLUGIN.md says, ending "then run pytest". It said to add the tool to `MODULES` in `src/PKG/tools/__init__.py`, a file the scaffold does not write and a list a plugin does not have, and to run `make test` and `make readme`, this repository's targets. CONTRIBUTING.md, the README and WRITING_A_PLUGIN.md now document the two flags.

## [0.1.0] - Unreleased

### Added

- A read-only MCP server on FastMCP 3.4.7, over stdio (the default, so `uvx marketlens-mcp` works) or streamable HTTP on 127.0.0.1 / ::1 with a bearer token.
- `marketlens_schema`: vendor-neutral canonical models (schema 1.0.0) for market, reference, brokerage and analytics data, with units on every numeric field, UTC timestamps, exact decimals, a reason for every absent value and a provenance block on every response; JSON Schema export.
- Capabilities the owner switches on and off in `~/.config/marketlens/config.yaml` (market, reference, news, analytics on; portfolio, results.export and provider.docs off; results always on), with one-line refusals for invalid settings.
- A DuckDB-over-Parquet result store: results above 200 rows or about 6,000 tokens are stored and returned as a marker (typed columns, preview, provenance, pagination, three ready-made queries); per-session folders, 24-hour lifetime, 5 GB cap with oldest-first eviction.
- Results tools: `results_query` (one guarded read-only SELECT with a forced LIMIT, run in a locked-down DuckDB), `results_describe`, `results_sample`, `results_list`, `results_drop`, and `results_export` (off by default).
- A trust envelope on every tool result; third-party text carries an untrusted-text notice.
- A plugin API (1.0) through the `marketlens.plugins` entry-point group: plugins load only when enabled, register atomically, and a broken plugin is skipped with a reason.
- The console script: `serve`, `tools [--json|--markdown] [--all]`, `capabilities`, `plugins`, `config path|show|init`, `doctor [--network]`, `schema`, `results list|purge`, `add-capability`, `readme`, `--version`.
- `marketlens_mcp.testing` (`temp_store`, `make_context`, `call_tool`) for plugin authors.
